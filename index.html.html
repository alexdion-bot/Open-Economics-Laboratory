About

Purpose and scope.

The Laboratory produces empirical research. It does not issue policy recommendations.

The Laboratory was founded in 2026 as an independent research group. Membership is open to undergraduate, graduate and PhD students in economics.

Activity is organised around a monthly seminar and a working paper series. Drafts are presented, discussed by assigned discussants, revised, and then released together with the code and data used to produce them.

Subject coverage is broad and standards of evidence are fixed. A result is published whether or not it confirms the initial hypothesis, including when the estimate is imprecise or the effect is not distinguishable from zero.

Fields

Fields of research.

Fields are not assigned. Members propose their own questions and the group reviews the research design. The eight fields above are working categories. The Laboratory follows the Journal of Economic Literature classification for the record.

Process

Stages of review.

Stage one

Proposal

A note of two pages setting out the question, the data and the source of variation. Circulated one week before the session.

Stage two

Seminar

Ninety minutes, monthly, online. Two discussants are assigned in advance. The first half of the session addresses identification rather than results.

Stage three

Revision

The author responds in writing to each comment received. Code and data are deposited in the shared repository.

Stage four

Publication

One member of the advisory board reads the final draft. The paper is then released under a permanent number. Papers are not withdrawn. A revised version supersedes the earlier one, which remains available.

Network

Where members study.

Members are undergraduate, graduate and PhD students at the universities listed above. The Laboratory is run by students and remains independent. It is not affiliated with, hosted by or endorsed by any of these institutions.

Sessions are scheduled within the overlap between European, East Asian and North American time zones. Recordings and written discussant notes are circulated within 48 hours.

Membership

Applications.

Membership is open to undergraduate, graduate and PhD students in economics. Applicants send one paragraph describing the research question they intend to work on and their current institution. There is no membership fee.
